What changed, and why it is sharper

The liquid moved from the CPU to the GPU between revisions. That is the whole of the difference.

beforeA 168×168 canvas painted in JavaScript and scaled up about four times. Every ribbon edge was interpolated across four screen pixels before it reached the page — soft by construction, and nothing downstream could recover it.
afterA WebGL fragment shader at full device resolution. Same domain-warped fBm — fbm(p + fbm(p + fbm(p))) — but every screen pixel is evaluated exactly once, at its own coordinate.
detailFive octaves instead of three. On the CPU the fourth and fifth landed under a pixel after the upscale and cost time for nothing; at full resolution they are the fine structure inside each ribbon.
edgesA smoothstep(0.16, 0.84) pass hardens the boundary between light and dark bands. This is what reads as liquid metal rather than haze, and it is only worth doing once the resolution is there to carry it.
costLower than before. The CPU version held a whole core at ~17ms a frame; the shader is a single full-screen triangle the GPU finishes in under a millisecond. Software fallback renderers are the slow case, not the common one.
chromaPushed 1.26× at the source. Blue loses saturation through frost, so it has to leave over-saturated — but less than the CPU build needed, because the shader is not also fighting the upscale.
